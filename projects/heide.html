Racism’s Impact on Social Determinants of Health Towards Marginalized Communities Abstract And Reflection

The HEIDE steps program was a very interesting and enjoyable experience. I had the opportunity to take advantage of it in the summer of 2026. The main topic that tied this whole thing together was the social determinant of health. The social determinants of health (or SDOH), is an umbrella term for factors in aspects of living, such as transport, environment, and education that could affect people's health. Each week we had both a webinar and an in person meeting discussing some of the several parts that affect an overall population’s health. Before this, every time I heard “healthcare” I immediately thought of doctors, surgeons, and the hospital, in hindsight this was very incorrect. HEIDE taught me that healthcare is a much more broad term than I had thought before. Instead of it just being about solving symptoms that are in a person when they are sick or injured, healthcare can also be described as something that solves the ongoing issue of sickness and injury. Not only that but HEIDE has let me experience firsthand At the end of this program, my group and I had to make a presentation about SDOH and to use what we did and learned in the program (volunteer hours and the knowledge of SDOH) plus personal experience to make an impactful and informative slideshow. The main topic of it was the impact of racism on SDOH for marginalized communities. The presentation explored how different forms of racism influence social determinants of health, including access to food, education, healthcare, financial stability, and mental health. Using statistics, we presented how racial inequality led to unfair treatment and generally worse conditions in all of the introduced factors. Along with presenting the impact of racism, we presented ways to confront and chip away at the issue such as phone numbers where one can report hate crimes, charities and shelter for the underprivileged, and public assistance for healthcare and finance. The big idea of the presentation was the importance of recognizing racism as a public health issue and working towards solving it. HEIDE has impacted me by showing me career opportunities, ways to be kind to my community, and gave me a future goal. By showing me several niches, it has widened my views, letting me know of possible paths that fit me more. This has especially been true for epidemiology which has piqued my interest. It has also shown me how much I enjoyed giving back to the community that I grew up in, as it is very fulfilling. I have been thinking of those who are less lucky than me, and I want to try to make their lives better. Lastly, it has given me a goal, to help spread awareness and knowledge on the issues that cause ailments in this society and to help those in need. I am currently continuing on this goal by doing the Sanofi Biomedical program where I am researching and helping with a publication that deals with Adverse Social Determinants of Health in Autoimmune Disease Development with Peripheral Nervous System Involvement.
