Investigation of ERBB2 Transcript Isoforms for Therapeutic Target Discovery in Breast Cancer

My project researched tumor-specific therapeutic targets that improved cancer treatment with reduced effects on normal tissue. Transcript isoforms could exhibit tumor-enriched expression and therefore represent potential targets with greater specificity. Erb-b2 receptor tyrosine kinase-2 (ERBB2) was a clinically established breast cancer target gene. My objective in the current work was to determine whether ERBB2 transcript isoforms exhibited tumor-associated switching and to identify isoforms with potential tumor-specific therapeutic value. I analyzed RNA-seq transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project using Python. I examined twenty-two ERBB2 transcripts. For each sample, I calculated isoform fractions as the percentage of total ERBB2 transcript expression, and compared mean tumor and normal fractions. The isoform, ENST00000541774.5, showed the largest difference in expression, increasing from 63.04% in normal tissue to 69.44% in tumors, a 6.40-percentage-point increase. However, this transcript was already highly expressed in normal tissue. Several other isoforms showed smaller differences, with no strong evidence of a highly tumor-specific ERBB2 isoform. In conclusion, I found that although ERBB2 transcript expression differed between tumor and normal tissue, the observed changes did not identify a strong association with the tumor tissue. I concluded that larger transcriptome-wide screens in the future might reveal isoforms with greater tumor specificity and therapeutic potential.
